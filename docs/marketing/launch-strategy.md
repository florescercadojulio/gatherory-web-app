# Gatherory launch marketing strategy

**Recorded:** October 21, 2026.  
**Status:** Proposed strategy saved from the marketing discussion.  
**Confirmed language direction:** English is suitable for launch; Spanish is
supported through localization.  
**Working assumption:** A small, founder-led launch. Geography, budget, launch
date, and the final public offer remain undecided. Targets below are experiments,
not forecasts or approved spending.

## Product context

Gatherory helps people plan events together, keeping ideas, preferences, tasks,
budgets, schedules, and invitations in one planning experience. Hosts and
collaborators plan together; guests receive a separate invitation and RSVP
experience. Main events can include related sub-events.

The product is iPhone-first and offline-first. The web provides account
management and a read-only planning companion, with separate advanced Blueprint
authoring capabilities. Decide records individual preferences; it does not
automatically approve an idea. Blueprints provide reusable starting points and
independent copies.

Older overview documents describe Blueprints as future work, while newer roadmaps
record substantial local implementation. Implementation, deployment, device
acceptance, and public availability must remain distinct in launch messaging.
The latest reviewed release audit still defers browser guest RSVP. Do not promise
app-free guest participation, Android planning, or dependable notification
delivery without confirming the actual release behavior.

The monetization direction is free personal planning and guest RSVPs, with an
organizer-funded Event Pass planned for collaboration. Billing is not implemented;
price, duration, and allowances are undecided. Do not advertise a final price,
unlimited usage, or a permanent free collaboration offer.

## Launch recommendation

Lead with one clear promise:

> Plan a celebration together without one person carrying every detail.

Start with small groups organizing birthdays, dinners, and holiday gatherings.
Recruit real planning groups directly and make the landing page demonstrate that
experience. English should lead the initial campaign. Test a dedicated Spanish
page and onboarding with a small Spanish-speaking cohort once the English
message starts converting.

## First audience

Target the person who usually makes the gathering happen: someone with an actual
event coming up, a few people helping, and details scattered across messages and
notes.

Recruitment profile:

- Organizing a birthday, dinner, or holiday gathering within the next 2–6 weeks.
- Planning with roughly 2–5 friends, a partner, or family members.
- The planning group uses iPhones.
- Already comparing ideas, dividing responsibilities, or tracking costs.
- Willing to try Gatherory for that real event.

| Audience | Priority | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Friends and couples co-hosting birthdays or dinners | First | Clear coordination needs, manageable events, relatively fast feedback |
| Holiday gathering organizers | First campaign opportunity | Timely reason to start; test Friendsgiving if the initial market is the U.S. |
| Bridal showers, engagement parties, celebration weekends | Next | Shared decisions and related events, with less scope than a full wedding |
| Couples planning an entire wedding | Later expansion | Higher expectations for guest logistics, vendors, seating, and reliability |
| Professional planners and rental-property hosts | Later | Different purchasing, support, and product requirements |

A person who only needs an invitation may not need Gatherory's planning depth.
Recruit groups whose planning workflow fits the current iPhone experience, while
recording mixed-device friction rather than assuming the broader guest journey
is solved.

## Positioning and message

Proposed positioning:

> Gatherory is the iPhone app for people planning a celebration together.
> Collect ideas, see everyone's preferences, and keep tasks, costs, and the
> schedule in one shared plan.

Emotional benefit: **I don't have to keep the entire plan in my head.**

Functional benefit: **We can see what people want and what happens next.**

Three messages should carry most of the marketing:

1. **Give everyone a say.** Collect ideas and use Decide to understand preferences.
2. **Share the planning work.** Keep responsibilities, costs, and timing visible.
3. **Start with a plan you can make your own.** Introduce Blueprints as reusable
   starting points once their launch availability is confirmed.

The earlier exploratory brand line, “Bring everyone's ideas together. Make the
gathering happen,” can support campaign creative. The more concrete positioning
above should explain the product and audience first.

Invitations support the story, but should not be the main acquisition promise.
Partiful promotes app-free invitations, reminders, and guest interaction; Apple
supports browser RSVP; Zola offers an extensive wedding suite. “All your event
tools in one app” gives people little reason to switch by itself.

The opportunity to test is how a small planning group moves from ideas to an
organized event. This is a positioning hypothesis, not a claim that competitors
cannot support collaboration or that Gatherory has established uniqueness.

Sources reviewed during the October 21 discussion:

- [Partiful](https://partiful.com)
- [Apple RSVP documentation](https://support.apple.com/guide/apple-invites/rsvp-to-an-event-devc9d9cdbd5/ios)
- [Zola planning app](https://www.zola.com/wedding-planning/app)

Recheck current capabilities before publishing competitive comparisons.

## First-user acquisition

Organize acquisition around **25 real events**, with roughly **100 participating
planners** as a working target. Recruiting whole groups tests the shared
experience. These counts are proposed learning targets, not expected conversion
rates or commitments.

| Focus | Action | Learning objective |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Founder-led recruitment | Find 10 initial hosts through existing contacts and introductions; help set up their real gathering | Does the product solve a sufficiently painful problem? |
| Host referrals | After a useful session or successful event, ask for an introduction to another organizer | Will users recommend it without an incentive? |
| Small hosting creators | Approach a handful of dinner-party, birthday, and entertaining creators with an actual upcoming event | Does demonstrated use bring qualified hosts? |
| Focused communities | Share useful planning advice and recruit pilots where rules allow it, with clear founder disclosure | Which problems and language resonate? |
| Search content | Publish a few occasion-specific guides connected to an example plan | Build longer-term demand, rather than relying on SEO for the first month |

Show a real sequence in creator and social content:

> Three of us are planning a birthday dinner. Here's how we collected ideas,
> chose our favorites, and divided the preparation.

Reuse one real workflow as a short video, landing-page demonstration, and App
Store screenshot story. Obtain permission for customer content and quotes.

Proposed first-month effort allocation:

- 60% recruiting and learning from hosts.
- 25% demonstrations and creator tests.
- 15% website and measurement.

Defer broad paid campaigns until a message produces active groups. No outreach,
publication, creator engagement, or spending has been executed by this strategy.

## Landing-page brief

The live page and local source were reviewed on October 21, 2026. Its restrained
visual style is worth retaining. The largest opportunities are the abstract
headline, lack of actual app screens in the hero, and the “Create an account” CTA
leading to web signup while the app is described as preparing for release.

### Proposed hero

> **Plan the celebration together.**
>
> Bring your ideas, everyone's preferences, and the details into one shared
> plan. Gatherory helps you organize birthdays, dinners, and celebrations with
> the people helping you host.
>
> **Get Gatherory for iPhone**
>
> Watch a real plan come together

Use the primary CTA that matches actual availability:

- Public release available: **Get Gatherory for iPhone**, linked to the verified
  App Store listing.
- Beta access available: **Join the iPhone beta**, linked to the working beta flow.
- No immediate access: **Get launch updates**, connected to a functioning signup
  and follow-up process.

Web account creation becomes secondary. Do not use a download or beta CTA before
its destination delivers the promised access.

### Page sequence

| Section | Recommended change |
| --- | --- |
| Product demonstration | Real iPhone screens following one birthday dinner through Ideas → Decide → To-dos → Schedule; place the first screen beside the hero |
| Three benefits | “Keep good ideas together.” “See what everyone prefers.” “Know who's doing what.” Pair each with product evidence |
| Interactive Decide demo | Retain it, using the same birthday-dinner example; explain “See everyone's preferences, then choose what makes the plan” |
| Starting points | Three relevant Blueprint previews and clear descriptions, if included in the release |
| Host and guest experience | Compress the large role explanation to “Plan with your helpers. Share the details your guests need” |
| Real-world proof | A permissioned pilot story or quote describing the event, planning group, and concrete benefit |
| FAQ and final CTA | Explain iPhone availability, guest requirements, offline behavior, and the actual launch offer; repeat the primary CTA |

Move terms such as “eligible planner,” “workspace,” and “approval” out of the
main sales narrative unless they help visitors make an adoption decision. Keep
the precise permission and preference-versus-approval explanations in help
content and relevant FAQs.

Replace the wedding-heavy venue walkthrough, flower, and reception examples with
one coherent birthday or dinner story. Keep weddings as a secondary use case.

For Spanish, translate the full conversion journey: page, demonstration captions,
CTA destination, and onboarding. Recruit a separate small Spanish-speaking pilot
cohort to learn whether the same message works. Localization does not establish
a specific launch country or acquisition market.

## Four-week launch experiment

Timing is relative to an agreed launch start, not a scheduled commitment.

| Week | Work |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Confirm the release offer, revise hero and CTA, prepare one real demo, recruit 10 hosts |
| 2 | Observe hosts starting plans and inviting helpers; find where collaborators fail to participate |
| 3 | Publish the first permissioned case study; run a few creator or community tests with dedicated links |
| 4 | Expand toward 25 events through the strongest source; fix activation problems before increasing traffic |

## Measurement

Primary metric: **activated planning groups**. Define this initially as an event
where the host and at least one collaborator each take a meaningful planning
action within seven days. Specify qualifying actions consistently before
instrumenting the metric; opening a page or accepting an invitation alone should
not count as planning.

Then measure whether the group returns to plan on another day before the event.

Follow the funnel:

1. Landing-page visit.
2. Beta or App Store CTA click.
3. Event created.
4. Collaborator participates.
5. Group returns to plan.

Website clicks do not establish installation or app use. Use campaign links and
a short acquisition-source question where attribution is incomplete. Compare
sources by activated groups and subsequent use, alongside qualitative feedback.
These are proposed measurements, not a claim that analytics are already wired up.

## Next decisions and stopping point

October 21 follow-up: the hero, preview CTA, and real product demo are implemented
locally in the landing repository, including English/Spanish homepage and iPhone
pages. See the [prototype handoff](../../../gatherory-landing/docs/launch-prototype.md)
for screenshots, verification, and remaining work. The site has not been deployed;
campaign recruitment, spending, and acquisition tracking have not begun here.

The subsequent birthday-dinner pass replaces the wedding screenshots with
Maya’s Birthday Dinner, adds sourced lifestyle imagery and pictured planners,
and fills sample account/participant portraits in the local development
database without a reset. The prototype handoff records validation and sources.

The next visual pass uses distinct photos for the main event, six ideas, and
four related events, with updated native captures and an Events gallery step.
Marketing keeps collaborative planning central while showing that people can
give each event and idea its own look. The image manifest records all eleven
placements and sources; the repeated stock backdrops have been removed.

Before execution, resolve:

- Initial geography and available founder time or marketing budget.
- Launch date and whether the offer is beta access, launch updates, or download.
- Features and guest-platform behavior verified in the actual release.
- The first 10 real hosts and their upcoming events.
- The demonstration event and permissioned assets.
- Launch pricing/offer wording, without implying planned billing is available.
